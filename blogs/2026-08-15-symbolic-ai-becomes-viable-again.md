As GPTs become more robust as agentic programmers and problem-solvers, symbolic AI becomes increasingly viable again. Symbolic systems were never necessarily bad at solving bounded problems; they were expensive to author, brittle to maintain, and painful to extend. But if a capable coding agent can inspect failures, identify whether a new edge case needs another rule or exposes a missing abstraction, refactor the logic, generate tests, and verify the result, then much of that historical maintenance cost collapses. Instead of running a huge neural model every time you need an intelligent decision, you can use the huge model to *manufacture and maintain* a tiny, explicit, specialized intelligence that runs locally for almost nothing.

That won’t work for every problem. You still need a domain that can be grounded into meaningful states, actions, constraints, and outcomes, and some reliable way to tell whether the system is behaving correctly. But wherever those conditions hold, agentic programming could make an entire class of previously uneconomical symbolic solutions practical again.
